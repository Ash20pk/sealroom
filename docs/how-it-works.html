How Sealroom works

Two law firms find out whether they share clients without either one showing its client list to anyone. Then the firm's AI assistant works with the conflicted clients walled off. Every step leaves a receipt anyone can check.

Built on NEAR + NEAR AI Cloud
Overview, November 2026 · Page 1 of 2

The problem

When a lawyer moves firms, both firms must check for conflicts of interest: is the lawyer's old client someone the new firm is suing, or acting for? Today one side hands its confidential client list to the other, or to a vendor, and simply has to trust them.

The idea

Each firm locks its list in its own browser and drops it into a sealed room: a computer chip that even its operator cannot look inside. Only the matches come out. A public notary (the NEAR blockchain) checks everyone's signatures and keeps a receipt.

1The conflict check

  1. Check the room is genuineIntel's certificate proves the chip is real, sealed and unmodified.
  2. Lock and signEach firm scrambles its list in its own browser and signs it with its registered key.
  3. Secret handshakeExact name matches ("Vale Doce S.A." = "Vale Doce") are found by maths alone.
  4. The sealed AI looks deeperIt answers with row numbers only, never names, so nothing else can leak.
  5. The notary records itNEAR refuses the record unless every signature checks out.
  6. Ranked result"Old client vs. the new firm's opponent" comes first, then cases to review.

2Everyone has an ID card

🏛Firm

Registers its own signing key. A list it didn't sign is rejected.

⚖Lawyer

Own NEAR account, added by the firm. Signs every AI session they start.

🤖AI agent

Owned by a partner, locked to one AI model and one set of instructions. Its key can do exactly one thing.

Like a building pass, with NEAR as the front desk that checks it every time. A firm can cancel a pass at any moment.

3The AI assistant, with walls

Conflicted clients from the check become walls. Every blocked read gets the same refusal, so the AI can't work out who the firm acts for.

4Why you don't have to trust us

No list ever leaves a firm unscrambled. Our server only sees row numbers.
Every result comes with a proof pack anyone can replay to get the same answer.
Each firm can later prove exactly which list it submitted, without showing it.
The notary rejects anything with a missing or forged signature.
Tampering with the result, the AI's instructions or a reply is detected.
The AI agent can't swap its model or instructions, or act without a lawyer's signature.
Sealed room (enclave)A sealed processor whose memory is encrypted. Intel signs a certificate saying exactly what runs inside.
NEARA public blockchain. Here it is a notary: it stores receipts and checks signatures, but never sees client names.
SignatureA digital stamp only the key's owner can make, and anyone can check against their registered ID.
FingerprintA short code computed from a list. It proves which list was used but can't be turned back into the list.
